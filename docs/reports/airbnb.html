References ## Introduction [ go back to the top ] In recent years, short-term rental platforms such as Airbnb have reshaped the relationship between urban housing markets and tourism. By allowing short-term letting, Airbnb can generate higher rental returns than long-term leasing. At the same time, it has raised concerns about housing affordability, inequality, and the displacement of long-term residents. Earlier studies show that the growth of Airbnb is linked to higher housing prices and rents in major cities (Congiu, Pino and Rondi, 2022). The platform has also moved away from its early image as part of the sharing economy. Professional hosts and multi-listing operators now play a larger role, which increases competition for limited housing resources (Bosma and van Doorn, 2024). As a result, Airbnb listings are not evenly distributed across space. They tend to cluster in areas with strong demand, high density, and signs of gentrification (Shabrina, Arcaute and Batty, 2019).

Tourism-related factors help shape these patterns. Proximity to attractions and visitor services can affect both listing density and pricing, which encourages stronger short-term rental activity in some areas (Rabiei-Dastjerdi, McArdle and Hynes, 2022). Airbnb data has also been used to study broader urban change, including gentrification processes (Jain et al., 2021). However, we still know less about what drives the activity level of individual listings. Much of the existing literature focuses on neighbourhood-level patterns rather than listing-level performance. It is therefore still unclear whether listing activity is shaped mainly by tourism-related spatial exposure or by listing characteristics and platform dynamics. To address this question, this study builds machine learning models to predict Airbnb listing activity in London and compares models with and without tourism-related spatial features. In this way, the analysis tests whether tourism exposure adds useful explanatory power beyond listing and neighbourhood attributes. ## Research questions [ go back to the top ] 1. To what extent can listing characteristics predict Airbnb listing activity? 2. Does the addition of tourism-related spatial features improve model performance?3. Airbnb Data This study uses the London dataset from Inside Airbnb, which provides publicly available information on Airbnb listings. The dataset does not contain directly observed occupancy. Instead, it includes an estimated occupancy measure based on review frequency and booking assumptions. In this study, that measure is used as a proxy for listing activity. Listings in the top 25% of the estimated occupancy distribution are classified as high activity. https://insideairbnb.com/get-the-data/ The threshold for this classification is about 92 days per year. Because this measure is model-based rather than directly observed, it should be interpreted as an indicator of relative activity rather than actual rental duration. image.png 2. Spatial Analysis Methods Tourism-related points of interest (POIs) are used to measure the link between Airbnb listings and tourism activity. After filtering the POIs into tourism-related categories, the data is projected into a metric coordinate system. Machine Learning Methods This study applies a supervised classification framework to predict whether an Airbnb listing is highly active.
